The problem

Too much to watch. Too little time to choose.

Somewhere in here is the one you’d love tonight.

Streaming promised more choice, and delivered it. Thousands of shows and movies spread across a growing stack of subscriptions, each one built to win your attention for its own catalogue.

The result is a paradox. People pay for more entertainment than ever, then spend their evenings scrolling instead of watching. Households can’t agree. Parents can’t tell what’s suitable. Subscriptions quietly renew for services nobody opens.

The real problem wasn’t a lack of options. It was the gap between endless options and the limited time people have to enjoy what they pay for.

Perspective

One evening. Five seats at the table.

ForgeCity started by sitting in every seat around the remote. Each one sees a different problem, and Queuey had to answer all of them at once.

The viewerWhat would I actually enjoy tonight?

Answered by a personal taste profile and picks that explain themselves.

The householdWhat can we all agree on?

Answered by Watching together, which blends everyone’s taste and rules.

The parentIs this okay for my kids?

Answered by content tags and kids’ profiles that never show what they shouldn’t.

The bill payerAre we using what we pay for?

Answered by “Are my services worth it?” so you keep what you use and pause the rest.

The fanWhen’s the next season? What was that show called?

Answered by Coming up on your services and Ask Queuey.

Inside the look-alike engine

Matching how a show feels, not just its genre.

Public movie databases describe a title in a handful of keywords. That’s not enough to know what it feels like to watch. So Queuey uses AI to build its own profile of every title from public sources, then matches titles on three layers, weighted toward how they feel.

Rules keep matches honest: cartoons don’t match live action, kids’ shows don’t match grown-up dramas, and a light comedy won’t be sent to fans of a dark thriller.

Built to be trusted

Privacy by design, not by policy alone.

Your ratings stay yours

A household’s ratings are private to that household. Recommendations are worked out for you, not sold about you.

AI reads public data only

The nightly AI work uses public show information. It never reads anyone’s names, emails, or ratings.

Safe by default

Encrypted nightly backups, careful confirmation before anything is deleted, and checks that flag problems before people notice.
