The bottom line

Households pay for more than they can find.

The typical American household pays about $828 a year for four streaming services, spends 12 minutes every time it sits down just deciding what to watch, and still can’t say which services are worth keeping.

Households don’t lack options. They lack a way to tell what’s worth paying for, so they cancel, re-subscribe, and cancel again, guessing each time.

Time lost, and why nobody knows what you want

  • 12 minutes deciding what to watch, every sitting, in the U.S., up from 7.4 minutes in 2019 (Nielsen, Gracenote)
  • 19% abandon a viewing session when their search fails; 29% of 18–24-year-olds (Gracenote, 2025)
  • Each service sees only its own slice of a household’s taste and favours its own shows. 33% say this fragmentation makes TV worse (Gracenote, 2025)
  • A household isn’t one viewer: 49% of adults regularly watch with someone else at home, and a third of parents say the kids pick (Olyzon). One shared profile blurs everyone together.
  • No service will tell you it isn’t earning its $17 a month. The fallback is trial and error, or waiting for a friend’s tip.

Inside the look-alike engine

Matching how a show feels, not just its genre.

Public movie databases describe a title in a handful of keywords. That’s not enough to know what it feels like to watch. So Queuey uses AI to build its own profile of every title from public sources, then matches titles on three layers, weighted toward how they feel.

74% In a blind test of 150 pairs, “More like this” matched a viewer’s own judgement 74% of the time. It has been on for every household since November 2026.

Rules keep matches honest: cartoons don’t match live action, kids’ shows don’t match grown-up dramas, and a light comedy won’t be sent to fans of a dark thriller.

Built to be trusted

Privacy by design, not by policy alone.

Your ratings stay yours

A household’s ratings are private to that household. Recommendations are worked out for you, not sold about you.

AI reads public data only

The nightly AI work uses public show information. It never reads anyone’s names, emails, or ratings.

Safe by default

Encrypted nightly backups, careful confirmation before anything is deleted, and checks that flag problems before people notice.

Execution plan

Building Queuey to solve it, in four stages.

Queuey sits above the services. It learns each person’s taste once and finds what they’ll enjoy, wherever it streams. The first three stages are done and live at queueyapp.com.

  1. 1 · Built

    The core app

    • One taste profile per person across every service, from their own ratings, ending four partial pictures
    • For Me: a short list for tonight, each pick with its reason, replacing 12 minutes of scrolling
    • “Are my services worth it?”: a verdict on each service, so the rest can be paused and rotated
    • Watching together and kids’ profiles: picks for everyone in the room, within content limits
    • Coming up: new seasons and new shows on the services you have
  2. 2 · Built

    Smarter matching

    • Queuey’s own profile of every show and movie: how it looks, sounds, and feels (emotion, tone, pace, style)
    • “More like this” that goes beyond genre: in a blind test of 150 pairs it matched a viewer’s judgement 74% of the time
    • On for every household since November 2026
  3. 3 · Built

    Ready to rely on

    • More than 1,800 automated checks run before every change
    • Nightly health checks with alerts, a separate test site, and verified email delivery
    • Ratings stay private to the household and are never sold
  4. 4 · Next

    Launch

    • ForgeCity LLC approval, then App Store and Google Play developer accounts
    • Production hosting and app security upgrades
    • Apps in both stores, with Queuey Plus subscriptions
    • New titles profiled automatically each night
